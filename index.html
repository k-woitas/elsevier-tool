Elsevier Full-Text Downloader

Retrieve article metadata including abstracts (CSV, JSON) and full texts (original XML, plain text) by DOI or PII via the Elsevier Article Retrieval API. Everything runs locally in your browser; the API key is only sent to api.elsevier.com.

Use this tool on your campus network or via your institution's VPN. Elsevier checks full-text entitlements by IP address. Open access articles are available with any valid API key; for licensed (subscription) articles you only receive metadata and abstract unless your request comes from an entitled IP range.
Observe the API terms, quotas and licence conditions. Use of the Elsevier APIs is subject to the API Service Agreement and quotas, and text and data mining on licensed content is subject to special licence terms. See the Notes below for details. At the University of Bern, please contact Digital Scholarship at the University Library Bern for questions about these terms and about this download tool.

Notes

  • API terms and quotas: Use of the Elsevier APIs is subject to the Elsevier API Service Agreement; text and data mining is governed by the TDM provisions. Elsevier limits requests per second and per week for each API key (see API key settings and quotas). On HTTP 429 the tool waits and retries up to three times.
  • Plain text: When full XML is available, the text is built from the sections of the article body (headings, paragraphs, lists). If that structure is missing, xocs:rawtext is used. Formulas (MathML) are kept as raw text only.
  • Licence terms for University of Bern members: Text and data mining on licensed Elsevier content is subject to your institution’s licence terms. Authorized users may mine subscribed ScienceDirect content via the Elsevier APIs for academic research only; crawlers and scraping are not permitted. Results may be stored on internal systems for authorized users, but the downloaded dataset must be deleted when the project ends. Hosting the dataset with third parties, including cloud services, requires Elsevier’s written permission. Results may be shared with the required licence notice, but not used commercially or to compete with Elsevier products. AI use is only permitted with closed tools (self-hosted, or not trained on your input) and nothing may become available to third parties. Open access content under a CC licence is governed solely by that licence. This is only a rough summary: please discuss your project with Digital Scholarship at the University Library Bern before you start.
